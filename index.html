Postdoctoral Researcher · Charles University, Prague

Halil Mert Solak, PhD

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Zoology at Charles University, Prague, studying how diet and the gut microbiome shape the ecology and diversification of wild animals. I combine fieldwork with large-scale metabarcoding (16S and 18S rRNA) and statistical modelling to link host ecology, genetics and microbial communities.

My current work follows two systems: the adaptive radiation of murine rodents in Papua New Guinea, where I ask how dietary niche partitioning and microbial shifts accompany diversification; and sympatric Darevskia lizards, where I compare the microbiota of a parthenogenetic species with its sexual ancestor.

During my MSc and PhD at Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University, I studied adaptation to high altitude in Anatolian blind mole rats, integrating population genetics, innate immunity, diet and the gut microbiome.

Ongoing · Papua New Guinea

Trophic and Microbial Niche Dynamics of Two Subsequent Radiations of Murine Rodents on the Island of New Guinea

How do ecological opportunity and incumbency shape diversification? I compare the diets and gut microbiota of two waves of murine rodents that colonized New Guinea, the Hydromyini and the Rattini, across steep altitudinal gradients.

Ongoing · Georgia

Microbiota Composition in Sympatric Darevskia Lizards: Comparing Parthenogenetic and Parental Species

Rock lizards of the genus Darevskia offer a natural laboratory: I compare the gut microbiota of the parthenogenetic D. dahli with its sympatric paternal ancestor D. portschinskii to see how host genome architecture shapes the microbiome.
